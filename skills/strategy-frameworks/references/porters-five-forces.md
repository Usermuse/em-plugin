# Porter's Five Forces

Structural attractiveness of an industry via five forces. Position matters more than the industry: the same market is attractive for some players and not others.

## Grounding rule
- **Buyer Power** is customer-derived → fill from `evidence` (do customers switch easily? are they price-sensitive? do they feel locked in?), quoted and cited.
- **Competitive Rivalry, Supplier Power, Threat of Substitutes, Threat of New Entrants** are market-facing → fill from `context` and, secondarily, `list_competitors`/`get_competitor_capabilities` (label secondary), each cited.

## The five forces (rate each High / Medium / Low, cited)
1. **Competitive Rivalry** — number/parity of rivals, growth, differentiation, fixed/exit costs, price competition.
2. **Supplier Power** — supplier concentration, switching costs, backward-integration threat, uniqueness.
3. **Buyer Power** — buyer concentration, switching costs, undifferentiation, price sensitivity, information. **Ground in customer evidence.**
4. **Threat of Substitutes** — existence/quality/price-performance of alternatives, switching costs, integration depth.
5. **Threat of New Entrants** — entry barriers (capital, IP, expertise), scale/network effects, brand loyalty, regulation.

## Output process
Rate each force with its supporting result → rate overall attractiveness → identify the 2–3 most binding forces → strategic responses (reduce high-power forces, exploit weak ones) → positioning opportunities. Strategy should directly address the highest force.
